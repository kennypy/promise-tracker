# Why This Exists

PolitiFact rates statements as true or false. That's journalism.

This is infrastructure.

## The Difference

| What exists today | What this is |
|---|---|
| Journalists fact-check *some* claims *sometimes* | Every public commitment is recorded permanently |
| Ratings are subjective ("Mostly True") | The delta is measured: you said X by Y, here's what happened |
| Coverage is driven by news cycles | The database doesn't forget when the news moves on |
| Individual articles, not queryable data | Structured, searchable, comparable data |
| No aggregate scoring | Cumulative accountability scores over time |

## The Core Insight

We track everything that matters to us with precision:
- Weather: measured to the tenth of a degree
- Markets: tracked to the penny, every millisecond
- Sports: ERA, WAR, PER, xG — absurdly granular statistics

But **whether power does what it says it will do**? That we leave to memory. And memory is short.

## What This Is Not

- **Not gotcha journalism.** The goal isn't to embarrass anyone. It's to create a permanent record.
- **Not partisan.** Every promise from every side gets tracked the same way.
- **Not opinion.** A promise is either kept, broken, in progress, or expired. The evidence speaks.
- **Not punishment.** Some promises *should* be broken when circumstances change. The system tracks that too — with context.

## The Accountability Score

A person or institution's score isn't a grade. It's a ratio:
- Promises made vs. promises kept
- Weighted by: specificity (vague promises score less), magnitude, and time horizon
- Adjusted for: acknowledged pivots (changing course openly vs. quietly dropping a commitment)

The most damaging behavior isn't breaking a promise — it's *pretending you never made it*.
